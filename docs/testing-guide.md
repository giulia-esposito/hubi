# Hubi Prototype — Testing Guide

For internal reviewers and Revenue reps taking part in this validation phase.

## What this is

Hubi is Wellhub Revenue's AI Copilot, still an **internal prototype**. It answers Revenue questions and business scenarios grounded in the real Revenue Knowledge Center content (currently 15 real documents, WorkRamp training material). This test is here to find out whether it's actually *useful and trustworthy* for real Revenue work — not just whether it technically works.

**Always verify anything that matters against the cited sources.** The interface says this too, as a permanent reminder — it's not boilerplate.

## How this test session works

This is a **facilitator-led, single-machine test**. Hubi is not installed on your own device and is not reachable from it — you'll either be handed the keyboard/mouse on the facilitator's machine, or the facilitator will remote into your machine to run it there. Either way, only one machine is actually running Hubi.

**If you are the facilitator:** hand over control of the screen and let the rep drive. Do not explain how to use Hubi up front — the point of this test is to see whether the experience is understandable *without* guidance. Only step in if the rep becomes fully blocked (stuck, not just briefly unsure). Take notes on where they hesitated, what they clicked without prompting, and anything they said out loud.

**If you are the rep being tested:** just use it the way you'd use any tool you were handed for the first time. There's no wrong way to start — ask a real question you'd actually want answered in your day-to-day work.

## Suggested flow (not a script)

1. Ask a direct question you already know the answer to, so you can judge the answer against what you know.
2. Ask a business scenario — describe a real situation (a client, a deal, a process) rather than a keyword search.
3. If Hubi asks you a clarifying question, answer it and see whether the follow-up actually uses what you told it.
4. Try something you suspect Hubi *won't* have grounded knowledge on, and see how it handles that.
5. Use the **Not helpful** button at least once if anything falls short — the short comment box that appears is genuinely useful, even one sentence.
6. Use **New session** to see what starting over feels like.

## What to pay attention to (and tell the facilitator afterward)

- **Answer usefulness** — did it actually help you move forward, or just return information?
- **Accuracy against the source** — click through a citation. Does the source actually say what Hubi said it says?
- **Citation usefulness** — were the sources specific enough to be worth opening, or just noise?
- **Trust** — would you act on this answer for real work, or double-check everything regardless?
- **Ease of use** — could you tell what to do without being told?
- **Real-workflow fit** — would you actually reach for this in your day-to-day work, or is it a nice demo and nothing more?

## A visual detail worth knowing about

Some answers show a small label: **"Hubi needs more context"** (when it's asking you something back) or **"No grounded answer found"** (when it's telling you honestly it doesn't have the knowledge). These labels come from Hubi's own read of what kind of answer it just gave — they're not independently checked. If a label looks wrong (e.g. it clearly gave you a real answer but shows "needs more context," or vice versa), **that's worth flagging** — it's exactly the kind of thing this test phase is meant to catch.

## Giving feedback

Every answer has **Helpful** / **Not helpful** buttons. Click one on anything that got a real answer (not required, but genuinely useful data). Not helpful reveals an optional short comment box — a sentence on *what* was wrong is much more useful than none at all.

## What is and isn't captured

Every question, answer, its sources, and your feedback are saved locally to a plain log file on the test machine — nothing leaves that machine, nothing is tied to your identity beyond a random session code generated by the browser. Nothing about who you are is recorded. If you'd rather not have a specific exchange logged at all, tell the facilitator — they can point you to how to clear it.
